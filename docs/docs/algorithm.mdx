# How it works

There is no magic here, and that is deliberate. This page walks through what
actually happens to a piece of text, and then shows what each part of it is
worth — measured, on a corpus you can read.

## Two layers, doing different jobs

The hard truth about moderation is that the two things you need are not
available from the same place.

You need something **instant**: it runs in the browser while somebody is still
typing, it costs nothing, and it works offline. That has to be a matcher — a
list of words and some careful string handling. A matcher will never understand
context, so it will never know that "killer deal" is fine and "I'll kill you"
is not.

And you need something that **understands**: a classifier that can read "I hate
black people" and see hate speech in a sentence containing no listed word. That
costs a network round trip and a vendor relationship.

So moderato runs both, cheap one first, and stops as soon as the cheap one is
sure. Deliberate abuse never reaches your vendor bill; everything ambiguous
still gets a real opinion.

### The two hard ones

**The Scunthorpe problem** is the reason matching is whole-token by default. If
you search for listed words as substrings, you flag a town in Lincolnshire, the
word "classic", "assassin", "cockpit" and "Dickinson". Every filter that has
ever embarrassed a company in public got this wrong.

**The username problem** is the opposite, and it is why there is an exception. A
handle has no spaces in it. "bastardcollector" is one token, whole-token matching
finds nothing in it, and that is exactly the shape abuse takes on identity
fields. So `scanFused` looks inside long tokens — but only for listed words of
six letters or more.

Six is the number that separates the two problems. The words that hide inside
innocent long words are all short: *ass* in assassin, *cunt* in Scunthorpe,
*cock* in cockpit, *dick* in Dickinson. The words worth finding welded into a
handle are long: *nigger*, *faggot*, *asshole*, *bastard*. One threshold, both
problems.

```ts
// body text: whitespace already does the tokenising
wordlistProvider(PROFANITY_PRESET);

// identity fields: there is no whitespace to work with
wordlistProvider(PROFANITY_PRESET, { scanFused: true });
```

The two off-diagonal corners are not equally bad, and treating them as though
they were is how moderation systems become hated.

Letting something through is **visible**. Somebody reports it, you see it, you
fix it. Refusing something you should not have is **invisible** — the person
does not file a ticket explaining that your filter was wrong about their town,
their surname, or their dialect. They just stop posting. That is why the default
policy publishes anything it is not sure about, and why precision is the first
number on the [metrics page](./rehearsal.mdx).

## The policy is separate on purpose

Everything above produces scores. Nothing above decides anything.

The decision is one pure function — `decide(result, policy)` — with no IO, no
state and no clock. Which means the identical decision runs on your server and
in your users' browsers, and the two can never disagree about the rules. It also
means the whole threshold sweep on the metrics page is one classifier pass and
fifty policy evaluations: one variable, held properly.

If you change nothing else, change the threshold to fit your own corpus. The
default is defensible; it is not measured against *your* users.

## Honest limits

- The matcher is **English and Latin-script**. The homoglyph folding maps
  Cyrillic and Greek look-alikes *into* Latin; it does nothing for text
  genuinely written in another script, and the wordlist has no vocabulary for
  it. See [Limits](./limits.md).
- Nothing here reads **images or audio**. Images go to the classifier; video is
  sampled to frames; audio is not read at all.
- There is **no per-user state** — no repeat-offender tracking, no escalation.
  That needs your database. The `sink` is the hook.
- A classifier score is **not a compliance program**.

## Reproduce all of it

```bash
npm run build && npm run rehearse
```

Every number on this page comes out of that command, from `corpus/*.jsonl`.
Fork the corpus, put your own users' text in it, and re-run — that is the only
version of this page whose numbers mean anything about your product.
